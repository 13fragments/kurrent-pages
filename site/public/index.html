Kurrent Pages

A held-out benchmark for transcribing German historical pages, 1565 to 1948

Abstract.An engine receives a raw page image and returns the page text. The 53 scored pages cover Kurrent, Sütterlin, degraded Fraktur, early-modern hands, registers, and marginalia, from 20 volumes in 10 strata. Each output is scored offline against a private, double-keyed transcription with normalized character error rate (CER). The transcriptions are not published, so no model can have them in its training data. Only aggregate scores are published.

1How it differs from other test sets

  • The ground truth is sealed. No transcription of these pages is known to be online. Common Kurrent test sets, such as READ 2016 and the Swiss Federal Council minutes, publish theirs.
  • The pages are broad. They span almost four centuries and ten kinds of page, from tidy Kurrent to tables, marginalia, and pages that mix print and handwriting. READ 2016 and the Swiss set each come from one series of minutes.
  • The size is standard. The 53 pages hold about 1,700 lines and 13,000 words. The READ 2016 test set has 50 pages and 1,140 lines.

3Submitting an engine

  1. Test your pipeline on the seven public practice pages. They come with transcriptions and run on your own computer.
  2. Request the page images from the gated dataset on Hugging Face and accept its evaluation-only terms.
  3. Run your engine on the raw images and build the bundle described in SUBMITTING.md.
  4. Send the .zip file to kurrent-pages@13fragments.com. For an open model on Hugging Face, you can send its model ID instead, and the maintainer runs it.

One scored submission per engine per 30 days. No human in the loop and no training on the pages. Your outputs and per-page scores are not published.

4Disclosure

13fragments maintains this benchmark and also develops Kurrent Transcriber 1B. Because of this conflict of interest, results for models built by 13fragments are self-reported and are not ranked.

13fragments scores every submission, its own included, with the same scorer and the published protocol. The reference transcriptions are private, so only 13fragments can compute scores.
